# Autonomous research protocol

The agent receives the complete question definition and produces one forecast with a brief, source-linked explanation. The host handles scheduling and retains private working records.

## Read the contract first

Identify the measurable outcome, named resolution source, cutoff, units, options, and fine print. State the qualifying event in one sentence. Examples include a formal notification, a published data vintage, or a specific date's closing value. A title and its resolution criteria can describe different stages of an event.

Check the remaining forecasting time and the outcome horizon separately. Use the API's actual bounds and grid for numeric distributions.

## Find the decisive evidence

Start with the named primary source. Record its latest relevant value and observation date. Consult a second source when identity, units, timing, or coverage needs clarification. Search results are leads; important numerical claims receive a source-level check.

For time series, distinguish complete observations from a newly opened period. For event questions, identify the present state, required transition, and historical frequency over a comparable horizon. Apply each source to its stated population and time range.

## Form a distribution

Use the current state and reference class as the starting point. Include plausible alternative paths and shocks. A near-term asset-price example can combine a current quote, complete close-to-close returns, and a wider-volatility component. Other domains receive their own appropriate model.

For binary questions, return a probability. For multiple-choice questions, cover every option and make the probabilities sum to one. For numeric and discrete questions, provide the full CDF on the supplied grid, retaining the required tail and per-bin probability mass.

Treat a missing source as a specific information gap. Spend the remaining research budget on the gap most likely to change the estimate. Use a concise uncertainty description tied to the evidence and horizon.

## Publish once

Produce a short public-facing summary: timestamp, target, probability or quantiles, decisive evidence, major assumptions, and source URLs. The local working process stays private.

Submit through the adapter. Use the original files when resuming a recorded attempt. Inspect uncertain remote receipts before another write. Follow the platform's private-note and single-forecast rules.

## Learn from outcomes

After resolution, record the outcome, score, forecast coverage, and any repeated source or interpretation errors. Adjust the common research protocol using resolved questions or the official testing area. Forward results determine whether the approach merits further use.

The human operator handles account registration, identity, and prize administration. Question research, estimates, and publication run through the autonomous agent.
